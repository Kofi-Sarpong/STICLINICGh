# Problem Statement

## Background

In Ghana, limited access to adequate information and persistent stigma surrounding HIV and STI services can discourage individuals from seeking testing, treatment, counselling, and other support.

The absence of a centralized resource can make it difficult to locate healthcare facilities offering appropriate services. Concerns about privacy and confidentiality can further deter individuals from proactively managing their sexual health.

## Identified Barriers

- Limited access to reliable HIV/STI information
- Stigma surrounding HIV and STI services
- Difficulty locating appropriate healthcare facilities
- Concerns about privacy and confidentiality
- Limited access to comprehensive sexual-health services
- Delays in communication about results, appointments, and updates

## User Scenario

The project uses **Kwame**, a third-year University of Ghana student, as a representative user.

After experiencing concerning symptoms, Kwame visits a local clinic and receives a positive HIV test result. In denial and confusion, he seeks a second opinion and comprehensive services. Concerned about stigma within his university community, he searches online for alternative facilities but struggles to find information about comprehensive services and nearby clinics.

## Design Need

The proposed solution should:

1. Provide accessible HIV/STI information.
2. Help users locate relevant healthcare facilities.
3. Provide counselling and support.
4. Support appointment scheduling.
5. Address privacy and confidentiality concerns.
6. Reduce barriers to proactive engagement with sexual health.

## Intended Outcome

STICLINICGh was conceived to make sexual-health information and services more accessible and user-centered while addressing barriers associated with stigma, fragmented information, privacy, and access.
